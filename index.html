Social inequality and stratification shape people's life chances. Define the key terms, explain ascribed and achieved status, and argue whether ascribed status still matters more in Bangladesh.

Every society ranks people. This answer shows how, and who gets the better chances.

বাংলায় প্রশ্নের ব্যাখ্যা

প্রশ্নটা কী চাইছে? সমাজে মানুষের মধ্যে কেন বৈষম্য থাকে, আর জন্মসূত্রে পাওয়া অবস্থান (ascribed status) নাকি নিজের চেষ্টায় অর্জিত অবস্থান (achieved status) বাংলাদেশে বেশি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ, সেটা বুঝিয়ে যুক্তি দিতে হবে।

(ক)

সংজ্ঞা: সামাজিক বৈষম্য (social inequality) মানে সম্পদ, সম্মান বা ক্ষমতা সবার সমান না থাকা। সামাজিক স্তরবিন্যাস (social stratification) মানে এই বৈষম্য অনুযায়ী মানুষকে উঁচু-নিচু স্তরে সাজানো।

(খ)

উদাহরণ ও কেন: Ascribed status হলো জন্মের সময় যা পাও (ধনী পরিবার, লিঙ্গ, জাতি)। Achieved status হলো নিজের চেষ্টায় যা অর্জন করো (শিক্ষা, চাকরি)। দুটোর একটি করে উদাহরণ দাও এবং বলো কেন এটা সুযোগকে প্রভাবিত করে।

(গ)

বাংলাদেশ: ঢাকার ধনী পরিবারের সন্তান আর গ্রামের দিনমজুরের সন্তানের শুরুর জায়গা আলাদা, এই তুলনা দাও।

(ঘ)

যুক্তি: নিজের একটা অবস্থান নাও (যেমন শুরুতে ascribed status বেশি কাজ করে)। ২-৩টি কারণ দাও। শেষে উল্টো পক্ষের কথাও এক-দুই লাইনে বলো।

উত্তর সাজানোর নিয়ম: সংজ্ঞা, তারপর উদাহরণ ও কেন, তারপর যুক্তি, শেষে ২ লাইনের উপসংহার।

Definition
  • Social inequality is a condition where members of a society have different amounts of wealth, prestige or power.
  • Social stratification is the ranking of individuals or groups in layers (a hierarchy) based on wealth, power and prestige. It decides who gets access to resources and opportunities.
  • Ascribed status is a position you are born into, such as race, gender or family background.
  • Achieved status is a position you earn through effort, such as education or career success.
Example and explanation

Ascribed example: A child born into a wealthy family inherits money, good schools and useful contacts. Why I say this: the child did nothing to earn these advantages, yet they shape the whole future. This shows that ascribed status creates structural advantages (or barriers) that last across generations.

Achieved example: A poor student studies hard and becomes a doctor. Why: the position comes from personal effort, which shows that mobility (movement between positions) is possible.

How they interact: The two statuses are linked. A rich child inherits advantage and also gets the best chance to achieve more. Marginalised groups start behind, so the same effort brings smaller rewards. Age and gender also change a person's opportunities.

Bangladesh

A child from a wealthy Dhaka family goes to an English-medium school, studies abroad and joins the family business. A child of a rural day labourer attends a poorly resourced school and may drop out to work. Both are talented, but their starting lines are different. In contrast, a first-generation graduate who becomes a bank officer shows achieved status at work. Gender also matters: in many families a daughter still faces more limits on study, work and movement than a son.

Argument

My position: In Bangladesh, ascribed status still matters more at the starting line, but achieved status can reduce its power when education is open to all.

  • Quality education costs money, so family background decides who can build achieved status in the first place.
  • Social connections and family name help in getting jobs, which favours people born into advantage.
  • Gender and ethnicity create barriers that effort alone cannot remove.

Other side: Garment work, remittance income, freelancing and wider schooling have allowed many poor families to move up. So achieved status is real, but it works best for those who already have access.

Conclusion

Ascribed status sets the starting point and achieved status decides how far a person travels. In Bangladesh, the starting point still counts for a great deal.

The four systems differ in one key thing: how much a person can move out of the position they were born into.

প্রশ্নটা কী চাইছে? চারটি স্তরবিন্যাস ব্যবস্থা (দাসপ্রথা, বর্ণপ্রথা, এস্টেট, শ্রেণি) তুলনা করতে হবে। বাংলাদেশের জমিদার ব্যবস্থা ব্যাখ্যা করতে হবে। আর আজও দাসপ্রথা বা জাতিভেদ আছে কি না, তা নিয়ে যুক্তি দিতে হবে।

সংজ্ঞা: Slavery = মানুষ সম্পত্তির মতো, কোনো অধিকার নেই। Caste = জন্মসূত্রে স্থির, কঠিন ব্যবস্থা। Estate = সামন্ত সমাজে জমির মালিক অভিজাত আর তাদের জমিতে কাজ করা কৃষক। Class = টাকা, শিক্ষা ও পেশা অনুযায়ী ভাগ।

উদাহরণ ও কেন: গ্রিসে যুদ্ধে বন্দিরা দাস হতো, কেউ কেউ মুক্তি পেত। আমেরিকায় দাসপ্রথা বর্ণভিত্তিক ছিল। ভারতে দলিতরা সবচেয়ে নিচু জাতি, ১৯৫০ সালে বৈষম্য আইনত নিষিদ্ধ হয়। দাসপ্রথা ও বর্ণপ্রথা 'বন্ধ ব্যবস্থা' (closed system), কারণ অবস্থান জন্মসূত্রে ঠিক, বদলানো প্রায় অসম্ভব।

(গ)

বাংলাদেশ: জমিদার-প্রজা ব্যবস্থা ছিল এস্টেট ধরনের। অল্প কয়েকটি পরিবারের হাতে জমি ও ক্ষমতা ছিল, প্রজাদের সুযোগ ছিল সীমিত।

(ঘ)

যুক্তি: আইনে বিলুপ্ত হলেও আধুনিক রূপ আছে। যেমন মানব পাচার, জোরপূর্বক শ্রম, ঋণের বন্ধন এবং দলিত বা হরিজন সম্প্রদায়ের প্রতি বৈষম্য। উল্টো পক্ষে বলো, শ্রেণি ব্যবস্থায় স্বাধীনতা অনেক বেশি।

Definition
  • Slavery: people are treated as property and have no personal freedom or legal rights.
  • Caste: a rigid, hereditary system where birth decides social position.
  • Estate system: a feudal system where peasants work on land owned by nobles and give labour in return for protection.
  • Social class: a division of people by money, education and jobs. People in one class share similar lifestyles and opportunities.
Example and explanation

Slavery: In ancient Greece people became slaves mainly through piracy and war, and slave status was not always permanent. In the U.S. and Latin America slavery was based on race, so it was nearly impossible to change status. Why this matters: racial slavery made status permanent, and racial inequality continued after abolition through segregation and discrimination.

Caste: In India people were born into castes. The Dalits (the former "untouchables", about 16 percent of India's population in your textbook) sit outside the four main varnas and faced severe discrimination and exclusion. India officially banned caste discrimination in 1950, but caste-based inequality still exists in some places.

Estate: In medieval feudal society nobles owned the land, and that gave them higher status than peasants.

Class: People are often born into a class but can move through education or a better job, although moving is hard.

Key comparison: Slavery removes all rights, while caste keeps people in fixed positions. Both are closed systems because they depend on ascribed status. Class is the most open system because achieved status can change position.

Bangladesh

The zamindar system was an estate-type system. A few wealthy families owned the land, and peasants worked it. The landowners controlled the local economy and politics, so they held higher status, while peasants had limited opportunities. Why it produced inequality: land was the main source of wealth, and whoever owned it also held power over the people who depended on it.

Argument

My position: Slavery and caste have been abolished by law, but their modern forms still exist.

  • Modern slavery appears as human trafficking, forced labour and debt bondage, where people cannot leave their work. Your textbook says the Universal Declaration of Human Rights bans slavery, yet more people are enslaved today than at any point in history (it quotes an estimate of about 29.8 million).
  • Caste-type discrimination continues in South Asia, including against Dalit communities. In Bangladesh, groups such as the Harijan (sweeper) communities face exclusion in housing, jobs and social respect.

Other side: Legally, these systems no longer exist as official structures, and open class systems give far more freedom. But a law ending a system does not instantly end the beliefs behind it.

Conclusion

The systems range from closed to open, and class is the most open. Old systems like slavery and caste have changed form, not completely disappeared.

Q315 marks

Marx and Weber explained stratification differently. Define their key ideas, apply both to Bangladesh and argue which theory explains modern inequality better.

Marx looked at one thing, ownership. Weber looked at three: class, status and power.

Core wordsbourgeoisieproletariatclass consciousnessfalse consciousnessclassstatuspower
বাংলায় প্রশ্নের ব্যাখ্যা

প্রশ্নটা কী চাইছে? মার্ক্স (Marx) ও ওয়েবার (Weber) সমাজে অসমতা কীভাবে ব্যাখ্যা করেন, দুটি তত্ত্ব বাংলাদেশে প্রয়োগ করে দেখাতে হবে, আর কোনটা আধুনিক অসমতা ভালো ব্যাখ্যা করে তা যুক্তি দিয়ে বলতে হবে।

(ক)

সংজ্ঞা: মার্ক্স উৎপাদনের উপায় (means of production) কার হাতে, তার উপর জোর দেন। বুর্জোয়া = মালিক, প্রলেতারিয়েত = শ্রমিক। Class consciousness = শ্রমিকরা বোঝে সবাই একইভাবে শোষিত। False consciousness = শ্রমিক ভাবে সমস্যাটা তার ব্যক্তিগত দুর্ভাগ্য, ব্যবস্থার দোষ নয়। ওয়েবারের তিনটি দিক: শ্রেণি (আয়-সম্পদ), মর্যাদা (status), ক্ষমতা (power)।

(গ)

(ঘ)

যুক্তি: ওয়েবার আধুনিক সমাজ ভালো ব্যাখ্যা করেন কারণ তিনি টাকার সাথে মর্যাদা ও ক্ষমতাও দেখেন। তবে শোষণ বোঝাতে মার্ক্স শক্তিশালী। শেষে দুটো মিলিয়ে উপসংহার লেখো।

উত্তর সাজানোর নিয়ম: সংজ্ঞা, তারপর উদাহরণ ও কেন, তারপর যুক্তি, শেষে ২ লাইনের উপসংহার।

Definition

Stratification means ranking people into higher and lower layers in society. Marx and Weber both ask the same question: what decides a person's place in society? They give different answers.

Karl Marx (the economic view)

  • Means of production: the things used to produce goods, such as land, factories and machines. In simple words, the things that make money.
  • Capitalism: an economic system where the means of production are privately owned and the main aim is profit.
  • Bourgeoisie: the capitalist class. They own the means of production.
  • Proletariat: the working class. They own no means of production, so they must sell their labour for wages.
  • Exploitation: owners earn profit from the work of the workers, while workers get only wages.
  • Dominant ideology: beliefs that help the powerful keep their position, such as "the rich deserve their wealth because they work harder".
  • Class consciousness: workers understand that they share the same position and the same problems, and that they must act together.
  • False consciousness: workers wrongly think their problems are personal ("my bad luck") and not caused by the system.

Max Weber (the three-part view)

  • Class: a group of people with a similar level of wealth and income, so they share the same position in the economic market. In simple words, how much you earn and own.
  • Status group: people who share the same prestige or lifestyle. Status is the respect and honour society gives you, and it can be different from your income.
  • Power: the ability to make other people follow your will, even if they resist. In simple words, the ability to control decisions.
Example and explanation

Marx's explanation, step by step

  • Society is divided by ownership. Under feudalism, nobles owned the land and peasants worked it on the nobles' terms. Under capitalism, factory owners own the means of production and workers sell their labour. Why this matters: the person who owns the means of production holds the power in the relationship.
  • The two classes have opposite interests. Owners want low wages and high profit. Workers want high wages and safe work. Why: workers produce goods worth more than their wages, and the extra goes to the owner as profit. This is exploitation, and it creates a built-in conflict.
  • Ideology keeps the system going. The powerful control religion, education and the media, which shape what people believe. If workers believe inequality is natural or fair, they do not challenge it. This is how false consciousness is produced.
  • Change comes from class consciousness. Marx said that when workers realise they are all exploited, they will unite. He predicted they would overthrow the capitalists, end private ownership of the means of production and create a classless society.

Weber's explanation, step by step

  • Agrees that class matters. A person's income and property decide the kind of life they can afford.
  • But class is not the whole story. Status and power also decide rank. Example: a successful pickpocket may earn as much as a college professor, but the professor has high status and the thief has low status. Same income, different rank.
  • Power has a political side. It comes from membership in powerful groups such as company boards and government bodies. Big business and government are often closely connected.
  • The three often overlap. John F. Kennedy came from a very rich family, went to Harvard and became president. He had high class, high status and high power. But they do not always match, which is why Weber says each person has not one rank but three.

Bangladesh

Marx in Bangladesh

  • Garment industry (RMG): factory owners own the factories and machines (bourgeoisie). Millions of workers, many of them women, depend on wages (proletariat). Profit goes to the owners and the wage goes to the worker. Why this fits Marx: the two sides have opposite interests over wages and working conditions.
  • Rana Plaza collapse (2013): more than 1,100 garment workers died. Many people see it as a sign of what can happen when cost-cutting and profit come before workers' safety, which matches Marx's idea of exploitation.
  • Class consciousness: garment workers have held protests and strikes for higher minimum wages and better safety. Here workers see their problem as shared, not personal.
  • False consciousness: a worker who thinks "I should just be grateful for any job" or "poverty is my fate" and does not question low wages.
  • Land and sharecroppers: in rural areas, land is the main means of production. A landless farmer who works another person's land and gives away a share of the crop depends on the landowner, a pattern close to the old zamindar-peasant system.

Weber in Bangladesh

  • Class (money): a big businessman, a bank officer and a rickshaw puller have very different income and wealth, so their life chances in housing, education and health are very different.
  • Status (respect): a school teacher, a village elder or a freedom fighter's family may be highly respected even with a modest income. A newly rich person may have a lot of money but still not be fully accepted by old, respected families. Why: status depends on social honour, not only on money.
  • Power (control): a Member of Parliament, a union parishad chairman or a senior government officer can make decisions that affect many people. A government cadre officer often has high status and power even if his salary is lower than a big businessman's. Why: this shows the three rankings can differ.
  • All three together: many businesspeople also enter politics. A person who is wealthy, respected and in office is high in class, status and power, and this is how inequality becomes strong and lasting.
Argument

Strengths and weaknesses first. Marx is strong on exploitation and on why the owner-worker gap continues. But your textbook points out that he did not expect labour unions to give workers bargaining power. He also did not expect political freedom and rising prosperity to make many workers see themselves as individuals, not as one class. And communist rule collapsed in the former Soviet Union and Eastern Europe. Weber is more flexible, but he does not name one main cause of inequality.

My position: Weber explains modern inequality better, because it is multi-dimensional.

  • Modern societies have many groups, such as professionals, managers, officers and the self-employed, who do not fit neatly into only two classes.
  • Prestige and political connections change life chances even when income is the same, as the teacher, the cadre officer and the businessman show.
  • Power is a separate source of inequality. People can gain advantage through office and connections, not only through ownership.
  • Weber still includes economic class, so he keeps Marx's main insight.

Other side: Marx is stronger where ownership clearly decides power, such as factory owners and workers or landowners and landless farmers in Bangladesh. The huge wealth gap still follows ownership of land and factories.

Conclusion

Marx explains why ownership creates conflict and exploitation. Weber shows that rank also depends on status and power. For Bangladesh, Marx fits the garment and land examples, and Weber fits the wider society, so using both gives the fullest answer.

Example and explanation

Functionalist: Doctors and street cleaners are paid differently. Why: training to become a doctor takes years and few people qualify, so higher rewards motivate people to study and make sure important jobs are filled by skilled people.

Definition

Culture is the totality of learned, socially transmitted customs, knowledge, material objects and behaviour (your textbook's definition). It is an all-encompassing system that shapes the way of life for a group of people. It includes language, customs, beliefs, rituals and ways of understanding the world. Tylor (1871) defined it as the complex whole that includes knowledge, belief, arts, morals, law, custom and other capabilities acquired by a person as a member of society.

Example and explanation

Key features

  • Learned: through enculturation, we absorb meanings, symbols and behaviour. Example: a child learns to greet elders respectfully by watching others.
  • Symbolic: verbal or non-verbal symbols stand for ideas or objects. Example: a national flag stands for a country. Symbols are unique to humans and are central to cultural learning.
  • Shared: common beliefs, values and experiences unite people. Example: a festival celebrated by a whole community.
  • All-encompassing: covers daily practices, technology and popular culture.
  • Integrated: change in one area affects others. Example: when the economy changes and more women work, family structure changes too.

Sociological perspectives

  • Functionalist: culture maintains social equilibrium and cohesion. Shared values hold society together.
  • Conflict: culture protects the interests of dominant groups. The dominant culture is the most widely accepted and influential culture, reflecting the values of the most powerful. The dominant ideology is the set of beliefs that justifies their power.
  • Feminist: cultural narratives can continue gender inequality, and dominant culture often reinforces gender hierarchies.
  • Interactionist: culture is kept alive and redefined through everyday face-to-face interaction and the media.
Bangladesh

The Bangla language, Pohela Boishakh and Ekushey February are clear examples. A child of Bangladeshi parents raised in another country will speak that country's language unless Bangla is taught. Why this shows culture is learned: the child's biology is the same, but behaviour depends on the culture in which they grow up. Ekushey also shows the symbolic feature: the Shaheed Minar and the language movement stand for identity and sacrifice. Pohela Boishakh shows the shared feature, with the whole society celebrating together.

Argument

My position: Culture is learned, not biological.

  • People of the same biological background behave very differently in different cultures, so biology alone cannot explain behaviour.
  • Culture changes quickly across generations, which genes cannot do.
  • Children learn language, food habits, dress and values from their surroundings.

Other side: Humans do have biological needs such as food and shelter. But the way these needs are met (what we eat, how we marry) is cultural. Sociobiologists (building on Darwin's natural selection) say some traits, such as men as providers, are rooted in genes. Most sociologists reply that people's behaviour, not their genes, defines social reality.

Conclusion

Culture is a learned, symbolic, shared and integrated system. Bangla language and festivals show how a people's way of life is passed on by learning.

Cultural relativism: An anthropologist studying an unfamiliar ritual asks what it means to the people who perform it before judging it. Why it helps: the researcher sees the logic of the practice within its own culture and avoids unfair conclusions.

  • Ethnocentric: some people may see eating with the hand as unclean and eating with a spoon as the only proper way, or the opposite.   • The best approach is to understand first, judge second: learn the context, then evaluate by basic human rights, not by one's own habits.

Other side: Relativism protects minority cultures from being dismissed and prevents the arrogance of ethnocentrism. It is valuable as long as it is not used to excuse harm.

Conclusion

Ethnocentrism blocks understanding, and relativism promotes it. Relativism works best as a way to understand, combined with respect for human rights.

Norms, values and cultural variation. Define the terms, show how values lead to norms and sanctions, explain why subcultures and countercultures arise and argue how norms are changing in Bangladesh.

Values say what is good. Norms say how to act. Sanctions enforce them.

Core wordsnormsformal normsinformal normsvaluessanctionssubculturecounterculture
বাংলায় প্রশ্নের ব্যাখ্যা

প্রশ্নটা কী চাইছে? Norm, value, sanction, subculture ও counterculture-এর সংজ্ঞা দিতে হবে। Value থেকে norm ও sanction কীভাবে আসে তা দেখাতে হবে। উপসংস্কৃতি কেন তৈরি হয় তা বলতে হবে। আর বাংলাদেশে norm কীভাবে বদলাচ্ছে সে বিষয়ে যুক্তি দিতে হবে।

(ক)

সংজ্ঞা: Norm = সমাজের আচরণের মানদণ্ড। Formal norm = লিখিত, শাস্তি আছে (আইন)। Informal norm = অলিখিত (পোশাকের রীতি)। Value = কোনটা ভালো বা খারাপ, সেই ধারণা। Sanction = নিয়ম মানলে পুরস্কার, ভাঙলে শাস্তি। Subculture = বড় সমাজের ভেতরে আলাদা রীতির গোষ্ঠী। Counterculture = যে উপসংস্কৃতি প্রকাশ্যে বড় সংস্কৃতির বিরোধিতা করে।

(খ)

উদাহরণ ও কেন: বিয়েকে মূল্য দিলে ব্যভিচার নিষিদ্ধ ও বিবাহবিচ্ছেদ কঠিন হয়। ব্যক্তিগত সম্পত্তিকে মূল্য দিলে চুরির বিরুদ্ধে কঠোর আইন হয়। অর্থাৎ value থেকে norm, norm থেকে sanction।

(গ)

উপসংস্কৃতি কেন: জলবায়ু, প্রযুক্তি, জনসংখ্যার মতো কারণে আলাদা রীতি গড়ে ওঠে। Counterculture সাধারণত তরুণদের মধ্যে বেশি, কারণ পুরোনো সংস্কৃতিতে তাদের বিনিয়োগ কম।

(ঘ)

বাংলাদেশ ও যুক্তি: পোশাক, নারীর কাজ, পছন্দের বিয়ে নিয়ে norm বদলাচ্ছে। কিন্তু 'লোকে কী বলবে' চাপে বদল ধীর। গভীর মূল্যবোধ (যেমন বড়দের সম্মান) বেশি স্থির।

উত্তর সাজানোর নিয়ম: সংজ্ঞা, তারপর উদাহরণ ও কেন, তারপর যুক্তি, শেষে ২ লাইনের উপসংহার।

Definition
  • Norms: established standards of behaviour maintained by society. To be significant they must be widely shared and understood.
  • Formal norms: written down, with strict punishments. Laws are the main example ("governmental social control", Donald Black).
  • Informal norms: generally understood but not written, such as standards of dress.
  • Mores and folkways: mores are norms seen as vital to society's welfare (violation brings severe penalties, such as the ban on murder). Folkways govern everyday behaviour (violation brings little concern).
  • Sanctions: penalties and rewards for following or breaking a norm. Positive sanctions include a raise, a medal or praise.
  • Values: collective ideas of what is good, desirable and proper, or bad and improper.
  • Subculture: a segment of society with a distinct pattern of customs, rules and traditions.
  • Counterculture: a subculture that deliberately opposes parts of the larger culture.
Example and explanation

Norm types: Parking rules and the rules of a football game are formal norms. Wearing inappropriate clothing to school or work breaks an informal norm. There is no written punishment, but laughter or disapproval is the usual sanction.

Values lead to norms and sanctions: If a culture places a high value on marriage, it will likely have strict norms against adultery and make divorce difficult. If a culture values private property, it will have strong laws against theft and vandalism. Why: values are the beliefs, norms are the rules made from them and sanctions keep people following them. Values are not shared by everyone equally, and they change slowly.

Cultural variation: Even with cultural universals, cultures differ because of climate, technology, population and geography. Inside one nation, groups develop patterns that differ from the dominant society. These are subcultures. When a subculture openly opposes parts of the dominant culture, it becomes a counterculture. Countercultures usually thrive among the young, who have the least investment in the existing culture. A 20-year-old adjusts to new standards more easily than someone who has followed the dominant culture for 60 years.

Bangladesh

Informal norms about dress, speech, women working outside the home, and choosing a partner are changing. Many young people form subcultures around music bands, gaming, campus groups or online communities. The common informal sanction is social pressure, such as "what will people say?", which can be strong in close communities.

Argument

My position: Norms in Bangladesh are changing, mainly because of education, urban life and global media, but the change is uneven and often contested.

  • More women study and work, which changes older norms about roles and dress.
  • Social media spreads new styles and ideas quickly, so youth subcultures grow.
  • But older generations often defend traditional norms, and informal sanctions like gossip and family pressure slow the change.

Other side: Core values such as respect for elders and family loyalty remain strong. So it is norms and expressions that change faster than deep values.

Conclusion

Values shape norms, and sanctions enforce them. In Bangladesh, young people are creating subcultures and reshaping norms while deeper values change more slowly.

Culture changes through innovation and globalisation. Define the terms, give examples, show how global culture meets Bangladeshi tradition and argue whether globalisation erodes culture or creates exchange and growth.

Cultures change in two main ways: new things are created inside, and things spread in from outside.

Core wordsinnovationdiscoveryinventioncultural diffusionglobalisationcultural homogenisationcultural imperialism
বাংলায় প্রশ্নের ব্যাখ্যা

প্রশ্নটা কী চাইছে? সংস্কৃতি কীভাবে বদলায় (innovation ও diffusion) তা বলতে হবে। বিশ্বায়ন (globalisation) বাংলাদেশের সংস্কৃতির সাথে কীভাবে মেশে তা দেখাতে হবে। আর বিশ্বায়ন সংস্কৃতি ক্ষয় করে নাকি বিনিময় ও উন্নতি আনে, সেই যুক্তি দিতে হবে।

(ক)

সংজ্ঞা: Innovation = নতুন ধারণা বা বস্তু। Discovery = আগে থেকে থাকা কিছু আবিষ্কার (DNA)। Invention = বিদ্যমান জিনিস মিলিয়ে নতুন কিছু (গাড়ি, টিভি)। Diffusion = এক সমাজ থেকে অন্য সমাজে সংস্কৃতি ছড়ানো। Globalisation = দেশগুলোর মধ্যে সংযোগ বাড়া। Homogenisation = সব সংস্কৃতি একরকম হয়ে যাওয়া।

(খ)

উদাহরণ ও কেন: ফাস্ট ফুড চেইন, সোশ্যাল মিডিয়া ট্রেন্ড, ফ্যাশন, গান। প্রযুক্তি ও বিশ্বায়ন ছড়ানোর গতি বাড়ায়।

(গ)

বাংলাদেশ: কে-ড্রামা, হলিউড ও ফাস্ট ফুড আছে, পাশাপাশি পহেলা বৈশাখ ও মঙ্গল শোভাযাত্রাও শক্তিশালী (UNESCO স্বীকৃতি ২০১৬)।

(ঘ)

যুক্তি: ঝুঁকি: cultural erosion ও cultural imperialism। সুবিধা: বিনিময়, নতুনত্ব, অর্থনৈতিক বৃদ্ধি। বাংলাদেশে স্থানীয় উৎসব শক্তিশালী, তাই দুটোই ঘটছে। নিজের অবস্থান নিয়ে লেখো।

উত্তর সাজানোর নিয়ম: সংজ্ঞা, তারপর উদাহরণ ও কেন, তারপর যুক্তি, শেষে ২ লাইনের উপসংহার।

Definition
  • Innovation: introducing a new idea or object to a culture. Discovery means sharing a new reality that already existed. Invention means combining existing cultural items into something new.
  • Cultural diffusion: the spread of cultural elements from one society to another.
  • Globalisation: increasing interconnection and interdependence among countries.
  • Cultural homogenisation: the blending of cultures into a more uniform global culture.
Example and explanation
  • Discovery: the discovery of the structure of DNA, or of a new moon of Saturn. Why: it adds new knowledge to culture.
  • Invention: the bow and arrow, the automobile, television, Protestantism and democracy. Why: each combined existing ideas or items into a new form.
  • Diffusion: fast-food chains, social media trends, fashion and music spread across borders. Why: globalisation and technology make the spread faster. Ritzer calls the spread of fast-food methods into other areas of life the McDonaldization of society.

Challenges of globalisation: cultural erosion, cultural imperialism (one powerful culture dominating others) and resistance. Opportunities: cultural exchange, innovation and economic growth.

Bangladesh

Young people in Bangladesh watch K-dramas, Hollywood and Bollywood films, use global social media and eat at international fast-food chains. At the same time, traditions stay strong. Pohela Boishakh is celebrated by large crowds with traditional dress and food, and the Mangal Shobhajatra procession was recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage in 2016. Fusion is common too, with traditional music mixed with modern styles. What this shows: global and local culture exist together, and people often blend them.

Argument

My position: Globalisation brings both risks and benefits. In Bangladesh, it has so far created more exchange than erosion, but it needs care.

  • Risk: powerful foreign media and brands can push local language, dress and food into the background, especially among children who grow up mostly online.
  • Benefit: new ideas, jobs, technology and creative work come through global links, such as online freelancing and the export economy.
  • Evidence of resilience: local festivals and language are strong and still growing in public life.

Conflict view: Critics note that the flow is not equal, because wealthy countries produce most global media. This is why cultural imperialism is a real concern.

Conclusion

Culture changes through innovation and diffusion. Globalisation can erode or enrich a culture, and the result depends on whether people keep and renew their own traditions while taking in new ones.

Language policy and bilingualism. Define the terms, explain how functionalists and conflict theorists view language policy, link to Bangladesh and argue whether a country should support more than one language in schools.

This is the social policy topic that closes the Culture chapter, so it is a likely exam question. It asks what a society should do when its people speak different languages.

Core wordsbilingualismlanguagesubcultureethnocentrismdominant culturecultural survival
বাংলায় প্রশ্নের ব্যাখ্যা

প্রশ্নটা কী চাইছে? একটি দেশে যদি মানুষ নানা ভাষায় কথা বলে, তাহলে স্কুল ও সরকারি কাজে একটি ভাষা চলবে, নাকি একাধিক ভাষা (bilingualism)? এই নীতি নিয়ে ক্রিয়াবাদী ও দ্বন্দ্ববাদী দৃষ্টিভঙ্গি, বাংলাদেশের উদাহরণ এবং নিজের যুক্তি লিখতে হবে।

(ক)

সংজ্ঞা: Bilingualism = কোনো পরিবেশে (স্কুল বা কর্মস্থলে) দুটি ভাষা ব্যবহার করা এবং দুটিকেই সমান বৈধ মনে করা। Language = শব্দ ও প্রতীকের ব্যবস্থা, যা সংস্কৃতির ভিত্তি। Ethnocentrism = নিজের সংস্কৃতিকে শ্রেষ্ঠ ভাবা।

(খ)

উদাহরণ ও কেন: ক্রিয়াবাদী মতে ভাষা সমাজকে একসাথে বাঁধে, তাই একটি সাধারণ ভাষা দরকার। দ্বন্দ্ববাদী মতে ছোট ভাষাগোষ্ঠী নিজেদের প্রকাশের সুযোগ চায়, আর একভাষা চাপানো ক্ষমতাবানদের আধিপত্য। উদাহরণ: কানাডার কুইবেক ফরাসি ভাষা নিয়ে, সিঙ্গাপুরে ইংরেজি মাধ্যম কিন্তু মাতৃভাষাও শেখানো, চীনে তিব্বতে ম্যান্ডারিন চাপানো।

(গ)

বাংলাদেশ: ভাষা আন্দোলন ও একুশে ফেব্রুয়ারি দেখায় ভাষার অধিকার কত গুরুত্বপূর্ণ। আদিবাসী ভাষা (যেমন চাকমা) এবং বাংলা মাধ্যম বনাম ইংরেজি মাধ্যম স্কুল নিয়েও যুক্তি দেওয়া যায়।

(ঘ)

যুক্তি: মাতৃভাষায় শেখানোর পাশাপাশি রাষ্ট্রীয় ভাষা শেখানো ভালো। এতে শিশুরা ভালো শেখে এবং সংস্কৃতিও টিকে থাকে। উল্টো পক্ষে বলো, খরচ ও একতার প্রশ্ন আছে।

উত্তর সাজানোর নিয়ম: সংজ্ঞা, তারপর উদাহরণ ও কেন, তারপর যুক্তি, শেষে ২ লাইনের উপসংহার।

Definition
  • Bilingualism: the use of two languages in a particular setting, such as a workplace or school, treating each language as equally legitimate. A teacher may instruct children in their native language while gradually introducing the language of the host society.
  • Language: an abstract system of word meanings and symbols for all aspects of culture, including speech, writing and gestures. It is the foundation of every culture and carries a culture's norms, values and sanctions.
  • Bicultural curriculum: teaching about the mores and folkways of both the dominant culture and the subculture.
Example and explanation

The issue: In the United States about 21 percent of residents over age five speak a language other than English at home. The debate is whether schools should teach in those languages. Supporters want to ease the move into the host society. Opponents want a single cultural tradition.

Functionalist view: Language unifies the members of a society, so a common language is functional. For a long time the U.S. demanded conformity to one language, which fits this view. Bilingual programmes are also functional if they help non-native speakers learn the main language and work effectively in society.

Conflict view: Language minorities are a subordinated group seeking opportunities for self-expression. Many attacks on bilingual programmes come from an ethnocentric point of view, the idea that any difference from the majority is bad. These attitudes try to stamp out foreign influence, especially in schools.

Examples around the world: In Quebec, French speakers are 83 percent of the province and French is mandatory in education for most children, while a 1995 vote on leaving Canada was almost evenly split. Singapore uses English as the medium of instruction but lets students learn their mother tongue. China has extended Mandarin instruction in Tibet. Why these matter: language policy can both unite and divide a nation.

Evidence on results: Findings are mixed because programmes differ widely, but a review of 17 studies found that students taught in both English and their home language progressed better than those taught only in English.

Bangladesh
  • Language and identity: The Bangla Language Movement and Ekushey February (21 February) show how a language can become the centre of a people's identity. This supports the view that language is the foundation of culture.
  • Minority languages: Indigenous communities such as the Chakma, Marma and Santal have their own languages. Teaching children in their mother tongue while also teaching Bangla follows the bilingual idea, and it helps their culture survive.
  • Bangla and English: Bangla-medium and English-medium schools offer different opportunities. This is your own application, not from the book, but it shows how language ties to class and life chances.
Argument

My position: A country should support one shared national language and also let children learn in their mother tongue.

  • Children learn better when they first understand the lesson, and a gradual move to the main language helps them.
  • Language carries culture, so losing a language can mean losing a culture.
  • A shared language is still needed for national unity and jobs, so the main language must be taught well.

Other side: Bilingual programmes cost money, and some people fear they weaken national unity. But the evidence suggests that support for home languages does not stop learners from becoming fluent in the main language.

Conclusion

Language both unites a nation and keeps minority cultures alive. A balanced policy teaches the shared language while respecting home languages.

Stratification

Income vs wealth
Income is salaries, wages, interest and rent. Wealth is all material assets (land, stocks, property).
Prestige and esteem
Prestige is the respect an occupation holds. Esteem is the reputation a person earns within that occupation.
Objective method
The researcher, not the person, assigns social class using occupation, education, income and residence.
Socioeconomic status (SES)
A measure of class based on income, education and occupation.
Rossides' five classes
Upper (1 to 2%), upper-middle, lower-middle, working and lower class.
Horizontal and vertical mobility
Horizontal is a move to the same rank. Vertical is a move up or down (Sorokin).
Lenski's view
As technology produces a surplus, inequality grows, and elites share only part of it.

Socialisation

Gender role
Expectations about proper behaviour, attitudes and activities of males and females.
Freud
The self is shaped by parents and by inborn drives that conflict with social limits.
Piaget's four stages
Sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational.
Bowles and Gintis
Conflict view: schools foster competition through built-in rewards and punishments such as grades.
Peer group
As children grow, peers take over some of the role of significant others.
NORC
Naturally occurring retirement community, an area that becomes an informal centre for older people.

Culture

Culture shock
The disorientation people feel when they meet cultural practices unlike their own.
Material and nonmaterial culture
Material is the physical and technological side. Nonmaterial is customs, beliefs, governments and communication patterns.
Culture lag (Ogburn)
The period of maladjustment when nonmaterial culture is still adjusting to new material conditions.
Culture industry (Adorno)
The worldwide media industry that standardises the goods and services consumers demand.
Culture war
The polarisation of society over controversial cultural elements.
Argot
Specialised language used by members of a subculture.
Sociobiology
The study of how biology affects human social behaviour.
